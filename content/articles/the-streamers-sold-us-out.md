As a latch-key kid myself, I never imagined this world where I am firmly on "Team Standards & Practices" but I find myself lecturing my boys from the couch about the racist, misogynist kidnapping aesthetics that appear on the same carousel as Bluey. It's a shame, because our paid streaming services used to be a safe space for parents willing to hand over a remote and the pre-tweens learning to use it. Now we have to contend with creators (and their assets) whether we let our kids loose on the internet or not.

![An Apple TV+ card for Zhong Creator Essentials, S1:E7 "Zhong Uses Extreme Camouflage," rated TV-PG: two masked figures in the front seats of a car and a person with a taped mouth in the back.](/articles/the-streamers-sold-us-out-hero.png)

Even before Beck had opinions on content, I was working in the Kids profile at Netflix. An Archer and Milo later, I've also seen behind the scenes at Google Kids and Hatch. I've advocated relentlessly for episode-level search and curation. I don't think it's going to happen inside the platforms. So I'm [starting to build it myself](/case-studies/apple-tv-show-finder/)!

I chose Apple TV+ as the starting point because, pound for pound, I think it's the best catalog outside of PBS Kids. And, honestly, we love Stillwater. It's just a hobby, but I'd love any suggestions or feedback you have on [the finder](https://finder.parentpeptalks.com/). Is it useful? What could make it better?

My hypothesis is that parents, especially, need support to help them quickly find the exact episode that will avert a meltdown. Or *maybe* you've wanted help finding something else… a show that could help drive a lesson home. The ability to show-not-tell why what is happening at [school, on the playground, in their dreams] is normal and, importantly, can be overcome.

We've all tried to translate what our kid is begging to watch, that we halfway remember, into a search bar that Is. Not. Set. Up. For. This. (Is latch-key parenting a subculture yet?)
