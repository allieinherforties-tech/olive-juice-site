Every AI pitch you have ever sat through was about the frontier. What the newest model can do. What it will be able to do next year. How quickly the world is changing.

None of that is the question that matters for your organization. The question that matters is smaller: what can a modest slice of a model do inside the work you already do, on an ordinary Tuesday?

## The principle

> You don't need the frontier of what AI can do. A small, well-chosen slice of a model's capability pointed at the right problem does more for a restaurant or a research team than the flashiest demo.

That is the first principle this studio states publicly, and it is deliberately unglamorous. A lot of AI consulting is people geeking out about the future and selling a tool nobody asked for. The alternative is to talk about your Tuesday mornings and show what a new way of working means for your week.

## What 5% looks like in practice

- At Oliver's, a Chicago restaurant, the general manager didn't need to understand a data pipeline. He needed to know how busy the week was going to be, early enough to staff it and plan around it. The foot-traffic forecast pulls from the tools the restaurant already used: Toast, OpenTable, a weather feed, a hotel-occupancy tracker.
- At the Usher III Initiative, a rare-disease research nonprofit, the team didn't need a chatbot. They needed a way to run a slow, specialized search again and again, so a compound that can treat more than one disease reaches more patients.

Neither engagement started with a model. Both started with how the team already makes decisions.

## Why not use the whole model?

Because the frontier is a demo, and a demo does not plan your week. Our metrics are defined by the outcomes of the tools and workflows we develop: calmer mornings, easier routines, or value generated via time-back and revenue gained. A frontier demo has no metric attached. A workflow that fits how you already work does.

## How this shows up in a project

1. **Paid discovery.** A clear map of where AI can improve your workflows, based on your routines and the tools you already use.
2. **Project sprint.** A solution to a nagging problem: a defined scope, set against a turnaround timeline, that gets you results before you're asked to evaluate a transformation roadmap.
3. **Retainer.** If the tool earns its keep, it keeps running quietly in the background and the next piece gets built.

That sequence lives on the [Start a project](/start/) page, and the engagements it produced are in the [case studies](/case-studies/).

The flashiest demo of the month will not be the thing that changes your week. A small, well-chosen slice, pointed at the right problem, will.
